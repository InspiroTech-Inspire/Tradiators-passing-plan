Risk limits that keep you far from the firm’s

Time budget

60 days is about 42 trading days. Plan to finish in about 30, so the spare 12 absorb bad weeks and news. That is roughly $40 a day or $200 to $250 a week. If you fall behind, keep the same risk and use the remaining time. Never size up to catch up.

Per trade

Base risk 0.5% ($50). Up to 0.75% only on A+ setups once you’re ahead. At 0.5% you need about 24R of net profit for 12%, which is around 65 to 70 trades at 1:2 reward and a 45% win rate. That is about 1.5 trades a day.

Daily stop

Phase 1: -1.5% ($150). Phase 2: -1% ($100). Hit it, close everything, walk away. The firm’s limits sit at $400 and $300, and many firms count open trades, so judge by equity.

Phase 1 drawdown ladder

  • -2% ($200) from your high: risk drops to 0.25%
  • -4% ($400): stop for the day, review the journal before the next trade

Phase 2 drawdown ladder

  • -1.5% ($150) from your high: risk drops to 0.25%
  • -3% ($300): stop for the day, review the journal before the next trade

Phase 1 plan

  1. Weeks 1 to 2. Base risk only. Get to about +2% and confirm the platform’s numbers match your own tracking.
  2. Weeks 3 to 5. If you’re ahead, step up to 0.75% on your best setups. If the drawdown is static from the starting balance, profit adds real cushion.
  3. Final stretch. Within 1.5% of the target, take only A+ setups at base risk. This is where most phases are lost to greed or boredom.
  4. Hit +12%. Stop trading and request Phase 2 right away. No “one more trade” on a passed phase.

Phase 2 plan

  1. Same strategy, smaller size. Risk 0.4 to 0.5% flat. The target is the same $1,200 but you only have $500 of drawdown.
  2. Daily stop at -1% ($100). The firm’s $300 limit is a third of that, and the margin is the point.
  3. Aim for 35 trading days. It will feel slow. The maths still works and a pass is a pass.
  4. Never lift risk to rush. If you’re behind at day 30 of 60, keep the size and keep the rules.
